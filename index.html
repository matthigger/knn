KNN, visually

To estimate the label of a new sample, find the training samples closest to it and copy what they say.

k nearest neighbors

Goal: estimate the label y of a new sample (the query x) from labeled training samples (xi, yi).

Approach: find the k training samples nearest to x, its neighbors Nk(x). The estimate is their majority vote. The estimate is their average label. There is no training step: the samples themselves are the model.

ŷ(x) = the most common yi among i ∈ Nk(x)

Near means distance ‖x − xi‖: L2 (straight line) or L1 (sum of |differences|). A tie drops the farthest neighbor and votes again (k−1 nearest) until it breaks. Shading shows ŷ everywhere.

ŷ(x) = 1k ∑i ∈ Nk(x) yi

Near means |x − xi|. The orange curve is ŷ(x) at every x; the dashed curve is the true f(x) the samples were drawn around.

Try this

  • On Overlap, set k = 1. The regions break into islands around single samples. Raise k: the boundary smooths out and the testing error drops, while the training error rises.
  • At k = 1 the training error is always 0%. Why? Is that a good sign?
    AnswerEach training sample is its own nearest neighbor (distance 0), so it votes for its own label. A 0% training error says nothing about new samples: compare the testing error.
  • On Swiss roll, raise k until the arms blur together. Why does a large k fail here but not on Blobs?
    AnswerOnce the neighborhood is wider than the gap between arms, it holds samples from neighboring arms, and they outvote the right one. The blobs are far apart, so a wide neighborhood still sees mostly one class.
  • Drag one sample deep into another class. At k = 1 it claims an island; at k = 7 its neighbors outvote it.
  • On Overlap, compare k = 1 with k = 2. Why are they the same?
    AnswerWhen the two neighbors agree, both rules give their label. When they disagree the vote ties 1 to 1, so the farther one is dropped and the nearest decides, as at k = 1. With two classes, an even k adds little over k−1; odd k cannot tie.
  • Switch the distance to L1 and drag the query: the neighborhood becomes a diamond.
  • On Sine, set k = 1: the curve jumps to each sample's label (training MSE 0). Raise k and it smooths; much larger and it flattens out the hills. Which k has the lowest testing MSE?
  • Set k to the number of samples. What is ŷ(x)?
    AnswerEvery sample is a neighbor of every query, so ŷ(x) is the mean of all the labels: a flat line.
  • On Linear, look near x = 0 and x = 1, and past them. Why does the curve bend away from the line there?
    AnswerAt an edge every neighbor lies on one side of the query, so the average is pulled inward. Past the data the neighbors stop changing, so ŷ is constant: k-NN cannot extrapolate.
  • On Chirp, find a k that fits both ends. The slow left side wants a large k, the fast right side a small one.
